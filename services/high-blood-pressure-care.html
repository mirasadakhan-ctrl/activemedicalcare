High blood pressure care in Fairfax, Virginia

Discuss blood pressure readings, medicines and follow-up at Active Medical Care in Fairfax, VA. Call (703) 620-6221 to schedule.

A high blood pressure reading deserves careful follow-up, even when you feel well. Active Medical Care provides primary care evaluation and ongoing management in Fairfax with Dr. Nisa Masood, MD. Bring the readings and questions you already have; the doctor can help put them in context and decide what evaluation or follow-up is appropriate for your situation.

What to expect

The visit may include repeat blood pressure measurements and a review of your medical history, family history and current medicines. If you measure at home, the doctor can review your record and discuss how you take readings. A single result does not tell the whole story, and diagnosis may require additional measurements. You can discuss routines such as activity, sleep and eating patterns, along with questions about medicine side effects or costs. Testing or a referral may be recommended when appropriate. Before leaving, clarify what to monitor, how to share readings, when to return and whom to contact if you have concerns between appointments.

Who it's for

This service is for people with repeated high readings, an existing diagnosis or a recommendation to follow up with a primary care doctor. High blood pressure often has no obvious symptoms, so feeling well does not replace recommended monitoring. Routine appointments are not emergency care. Call 911 for chest pain, severe breathing trouble, sudden weakness or trouble speaking rather than waiting for an office appointment.

Preparing for your visit

Bring your medication list and available blood pressure readings, with the dates and times recorded. If you have a home monitor, ask whether bringing it would be helpful. Include nonprescription products and supplements in your list. Write down your main questions, including concerns about treatment or the practicalities of checking at home. Do not stop medicines or change doses without instructions from your clinician.

Frequently asked questions

Can blood pressure be high without symptoms?

Yes. It often causes no obvious symptoms. Measurements and follow-up help your clinician assess it.

Should I bring home readings?

Yes, if you have them. Record when they were taken and discuss your measurement technique with the doctor.

Does one high reading mean I have hypertension?

Not necessarily. Your clinician will consider repeat measurements and your medical history before deciding what it means.
