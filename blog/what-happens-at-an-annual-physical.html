What happens at an annual physical?

A practical guide to preparing for a preventive visit in Fairfax, VA: questions, records, tests and follow-up. Call Active Medical Care to book.

Published October 8, 2026 · General health information

A preventive visit gives you time to talk about your health when the conversation is not centered on one urgent problem. For many people, the hardest part is simply knowing what to expect and what to bring. This guide explains how to prepare for an annual physical, what to ask during the visit and how to leave with a clearer plan for follow-up.

Start with the purpose of the appointment

The phrase annual physical is often used for a general preventive appointment, but the exact visit should fit your needs. Your age, health history, medicines and previous results help the doctor decide what to discuss and examine. Ask how often preventive visits are appropriate for you rather than assuming everyone needs the same schedule. If you have a new symptom or an ongoing problem that needs attention, mention it when you call. The office can help arrange the right type of appointment and enough time for the discussion.

Bring the information you already have

Prepare a list of your prescription medicines, doses, vitamins, supplements and nonprescription products. Include allergies and the names of other clinicians you see. If you have recent test results or records from another practice, ask how to share them securely. You do not need to recreate your entire medical history from memory at the front desk. A short, organized list gives the doctor a useful starting point. Bring your photo ID and insurance card, and confirm whether forms should be completed before the visit.

Choose your most important questions

It is easy to forget a concern once the appointment starts. Write down two or three things you most want to discuss, such as a change in sleep, a question about a medicine or uncertainty about a recommended screening. Put the most important item first and bring your list with you. You can also ask for an explanation of unfamiliar terms. A question does not have to sound medical to be useful; explaining how a concern affects your day can help the doctor understand your priorities.

Expect a conversation as well as an examination

A preventive appointment may include a review of your history, measurements such as blood pressure and an examination based on your needs. The doctor may ask about habits, family history and changes since your last visit. Screening and vaccination questions can be part of that conversation. There is no single set of tests that belongs in every appointment. If a test is recommended, ask what question it is meant to answer and whether any preparation is needed. Do not fast or stop medicines unless your care team gives instructions.

Check the appointment type and insurance

Insurance coverage depends on the plan and the services provided. Ask whether the appointment is a preventive physical, a focused problem visit or another type of visit. For people with Medicare, an Annual Wellness Visit is different from a routine physical examination. Mention your plan when booking and call to confirm coverage. If you plan to raise an additional concern during a preventive visit, ask whether it could change how the appointment is billed. Confirming the details in advance helps set realistic expectations.

Leave with the next steps written down

Before you leave, make sure you know whether tests, referrals or another appointment are planned. Ask when results should be available, how they will be shared and whom to contact if you do not hear back. If instructions are unclear, ask the doctor to explain them again in simpler words. You can repeat the plan in your own words to check that you understood. Keep the instructions somewhere you can find them, especially if another clinician or family member is helping coordinate your care.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need blood work at every physical?

Not necessarily. The doctor recommends tests based on your history and needs. Ask what each test is for and how results will be used.

Can I discuss a new problem during the visit?

Tell the office when booking. A separate or different appointment may be appropriate, and additional services may affect coverage.

Should I wait for a preventive visit if I feel unwell?

No. Call to discuss the right appointment for your concern. For severe or sudden emergency symptoms, call 911.
